## Executive claim

**Analysis**

NEAR Intents now settles about 36.4 million dollars of public flow a day [raw/forecast.json#/series/intents_usd/last30], and 93.1% of that value [raw/forecast.json#/series/no_near_share/last30] moves between assets that do not live on NEAR. The people who move it pay a real toll: over the last thirty days the measured user-paid spread was 59.4 cents for every hundred dollars settled [raw/forecast.json#/derived/toll_split/rows/2/centsPer100Usd/last30], and integrator app fees alone were 47.8 cents [raw/forecast.json#/derived/toll_split/rows/1/centsPer100Usd/last30] of it. NEAR's own cut, the gas its Intents contracts burn, was 0.0918 cents [raw/forecast.json#/derived/toll_split/rows/0/centsPer100Usd/last30] per hundred dollars: less than a fifth of one percent of what users pay to cross the booth.

The forecast says the gap stays open. A year out, the spread users pay is forecast at 64.5 cents per hundred dollars [raw/forecast.json#/derived/toll_split/rows/2/centsPer100Usd/plus365/central], while the gas NEAR keeps is forecast flat at 0.0808 cents [raw/forecast.json#/derived/toll_split/rows/0/centsPer100Usd/plus365/central]. Volume can keep growing and the network can keep shrinking as a consumer chain, and NEAR the token will still be paid almost nothing per dollar settled, unless the fee switch the protocol announced turns into a visible, growing flow back to NEAR. That is the toll booth problem, and it is the number to watch.

## Methods

Each series is a daily series. The model works on the seven-day mean of the observed days (a day with no value is skipped, never counted as zero), on a transformed scale: ln(one plus x) for counts and amounts, the logit for shares, ln(x) for ratios such as the take rate.

Five candidate models: the last value (naive); a random walk with drift estimated on the trailing 180 days; a linear trend on the trailing 180 days; a damped trend (Holt, damping 0.900, smoothing parameters fitted by one-step squared error on a fixed grid); and the median of the last three. [raw/forecast.json#/method/config/trailing][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/phi]

Rolling-origin backtest: origins every 14 days from day 150 of the series; horizons 30, 60 and 90 days; the target is the thirty-day average ending at the horizon, scored by the absolute error of its transformed value. Skill is one minus the model error divided by the naive error. The model with the lowest error at 90 days is used for each series, the simpler model on a tie. [raw/forecast.json#/method/config/originStep][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/firstOrigin][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/horizons/0][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/horizons/1][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/horizons/2]

Bands: the fifty and eighty percent half-widths are the empirical quantiles of the chosen model's absolute backtest errors at each scored horizon; beyond 90 days they widen with the square root of the horizon ratio, which is an assumption because the data cannot score longer horizons. Calibration reports how often the eighty percent band held the realised value in a time-ordered check that sets the width from earlier origins only (the verdict uses it when it has at least six test points, otherwise leaving each origin out in turn). [raw/forecast.json#/method/config/horizons/2]

Simulation: 2,000 paths from a block bootstrap (block length 14 days) of the chosen model's one-step residuals, with seeds derived from the input hash c8f448d8d5f2. The crossover and the twelve-month totals are read from these paths. [raw/forecast.json#/method/config/paths][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/block][raw/forecast.json#/inputHash]

Verdict per series: FORECAST_WITH_SKILL when the model beats naive by at least 10% at 90 days and the eighty percent band is calibrated between 65% and 95%; otherwise FORECAST_LOW_SKILL. A model that cannot beat naive is reported as such and naive is used with its wider bands. [raw/forecast.json#/method/config/skillThreshold][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/horizons/2][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/calibrationRange/0][raw/forecast.json#/method/config/calibrationRange/1]

Days whose swaps are marked partial in source_coverage (provider near-explorer, dataset intents_swaps: 2025-11-09 oldest edge, 2026-01-27/28/30 quarantined pages, 2026-04-02 to 2026-04-23 explorer cursor jump, 2026-07-23 quarantined page) are left out of this series entirely, and so are the two days (2026-02-02 and 2026-02-04) on which a successful swap has no USD value (the day is incomplete by the research definition); they are listed as missing days and never filled with zero or estimated. [raw/manifest.json#/datasets/6/title][raw/manifest.json#/datasets/6/definition]
- New organic signers per day (first organic day seen since the window start): Organic signers (any size, tail and head) whose first organic transaction since the window start (2025-11-10) falls on that day. "New" means first seen SINCE 2025-11-10, not lifetime first-seen, so early days include returning accounts that had been inactive; the window-start day itself (every signer is new by construction) is left out and listed as a missing day. Head signers are a negligible share of the count (see head_accounts_break), so this is read as new long-tail signers. [raw/manifest.json#/datasets/3/title][raw/manifest.json#/datasets/3/definition]
- Head accounts around 2026-04-02: transactions in the 31 days before and after: The 12 head accounts (organic transactions >= 100 on a day) with the largest of their before and after totals. Before = 2026-03-02 to 2026-04-01 and after = 2026-04-02 to 2026-05-02, 31 days each. tx_* sums organic transactions on the days the account was in the head; avg_daily_* = that sum divided by 31. On a day below 100 organic transactions the account is not stored, so the averages are lower bounds for accounts that were in the head on fewer days than 31 (head_days_* says how many). [raw/manifest.json#/datasets/10/title][raw/manifest.json#/datasets/10/definition]

Overlap of the Intents and long-tail series: tail_tx does NOT contain Intents or bridge transactions, because those are removed before the head/tail split (tx_organic = tail_tx + head organic transactions exactly, checked on every day below). F2 therefore does not overlap F1 in transactions. [raw/manifest.json#/datasets/1/definition]

Fee inputs: Per UTC block day: the sum of tokens_burnt (yoctoNEAR, and the same in NEAR = yocto / 10^24) and gas_burnt over every row of execution_outcomes (transaction outcomes and receipt outcomes, incl. refund receipts that burn no tokens), the outcome count, and the day's mean gas price = sum of blocks.gas_price / block count. Gross tokens_burnt of the outcomes. Whether part of a fee is burnt or paid to the called contract: nearcore docs state a 30% contract reward up to protocol version 86 and 0% from 87 (docs/economics/SEMANTICS.md); the mainnet activation date of version 87 is not verified here, so no burnt/paid split is applied. [raw/fees/manifest.json#/datasets/0/definition]

## Evidence against

**Analysis**

- The forecasts are uncertain. In the time-ordered backtest only 50.0% [raw/forecast.json#/series/intents_tx/calibration/pooledForward80] of realised Intents transaction values fell inside the eighty percent band, so the bands are too narrow and every series is labelled low skill. - Gas is only one toll. The protocol's fee switch sends part of the front-end and protocol fees to NEAR buybacks, and our swap data sees that flow only partly (raw/fees/FEE_MODEL.md). If most of it travels outside the public records, NEAR's real cut is larger than this thesis measures.
- A small rate on a large base is still money: the forecast twelve-month gas burn by the Intents contracts is 33.2 thousand NEAR [raw/forecast.json#/derived/intents_burn_near_12m/centralTotal], about 113 thousand dollars [raw/forecast.json#/derived/intents_burn_near_12m/usdAtLastClose/central] at the last close, with the price held fixed. - Volume is the least predictable input: a year out, the eighty percent band for daily settled value runs from 22.8 million [raw/forecast.json#/series/intents_usd/horizons/2/band80/0] to 75.1 million dollars [raw/forecast.json#/series/intents_usd/horizons/2/band80/1].
- Part of the flow is now private and invisible to us, and every figure here describes the public flow only.

## Long-term economic mechanisms

**Analysis**

Interpretation. A booth that charges almost nothing can be a moat: if NEAR is the cheapest place to settle cross-chain intents, volume concentrates there, and a later, modest fee could be worth more than a high fee today. It can also be a leak: wallets and apps on top collect the toll, solvers collect the spread, and the network that provides finality is reduced to a utility whose token is optional for every swap. The fee switch is the hinge between the two readings. The thesis does not claim which one wins; it claims that, on the evidence so far, the value is flowing around the token rather than through it, and it names the checkpoints at which that will be visible either way.

**Computed**

- NEAR is paid when a transaction burns gas: a share of the gas fee is burnt, so the Intents contracts pay NEAR in proportion to the transactions addressed to them, not in proportion to the dollars those transactions settle. A larger settled size per transaction therefore lowers the take rate, and more transactions of the same size raise the NEAR burnt without changing the rate.
- The user-paid spread (the gap between the quoted and the executed price) is paid to market makers and the protocol's solver network; it reaches NEAR only if the receiving parties are NEAR-aligned, which this thesis cannot observe.
- Stablecoin movement between other chains pays the same gas as any other swap but carries little price risk for the user, so a rising stable-to-stable share can lower the take rate without lowering the activity.
- These mechanisms are stated as hypotheses to be checked by the checkpoints, not as findings.

## Reproduction

From the repository root `nearesis-app`:
- `pnpm thesis:data` re-freezes thesis/raw from the local databases (read-only SELECTs); add `--bigquery` to re-run the billed BigQuery cross-checks.
- `pnpm thesis:build` runs the forecast engine on the frozen data and writes raw/forecast.json, THESIS.md, FACTS.md, the static site, thesis.json and HASHES.json, offline.
- `pnpm thesis:verify` rebuilds into a temporary directory and compares every hash, including raw/forecast.json; it exits with an error if any file differs.
- `pnpm thesis:verify --dir thesis/archive/intents-vs-price-v1` verifies the archived previous version.
Input hashes are listed in the Source index; output hashes are in thesis/build/HASHES.json.
